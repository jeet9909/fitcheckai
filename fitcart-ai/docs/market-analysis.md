# Market Analysis

> **All numbers below are analyst estimates with stated assumptions, not audited figures. Every headline number is labelled `ASSUMPTION` and must be validated with primary sources (Bain/RedSeer/Statista/company filings) before use in a funding deck.** The goal here is a *credible reasoning framework*, not false precision.

## 1. Market context (qualitative, high-confidence)
- India is one of the fastest-growing online fashion markets: young, mobile-first, price-sensitive, multi-platform.
- Online apparel returns are structurally high (size/fit driven) — a cost retailers actively want to cut.
- Virtual try-on has been validated by Google (Doppl, 2025), Walmart, Amazon, Snap — demand is real; the **cross-store fit-intelligence** niche is open.

## 2. TAM / SAM / SOM framework (India-first)

> Method: top-down sizing with explicit assumptions. Replace bracketed `ASSUMPTION` values with sourced figures before external use.

### TAM — Total Addressable Market
*Definition:* global value creatable by AI try-on + fit personalization + affiliate on online fashion.
- Driver: global online fashion GMV (hundreds of $B) × the share influenced by try-on/personalization tooling.
- `ASSUMPTION`: global online fashion GMV ≈ $700B–$900B; addressable personalization/affiliate value layer ≈ 2–4% → **TAM ≈ $15B–$35B**. *(directional; validate)*

### SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market
*Definition:* India online fashion shoppers reachable by a mobile try-on layer + the monetizable affiliate/subscription value on their spend.
- `ASSUMPTION`: India online fashion GMV ≈ $20B–$30B and growing double-digit; monetizable layer (affiliate commission ~5–10% on influenced GMV + subscription) → **SAM ≈ $1B–$3B** value pool. *(directional; validate)*

### SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market (24–36 months)
*Definition:* what FitCart can realistically capture given freemium + affiliate + a small team.
- `ASSUMPTION`: reach ~2–5M installs, ~0.5–1.5M MAU, modest influenced GMV per active user, ~5–8% commission, plus Pro conversion 3–6%.
- **SOM ≈ $10M–$40M annual revenue potential at maturity of this window** — *aspirational ceiling, not a forecast.* Base-case business plan should model well below this.

> ⚠️ **Investor honesty note:** These are reasoning brackets. Do **not** present a single big number as fact. The credible pitch leads with *return-reduction value to retailers* and *affiliate economics per active user*, which are bottom-up and defensible.

## 3. Bottom-up sanity check (the number that matters)
Per active user per month (`ASSUMPTION`s, replace in pilot):
- Influenced purchases: 1.5 items/mo × ₹1,200 AOV = ₹1,800 influenced GMV.
- Affiliate at 6% = **₹108/user/mo** gross.
- Pro conversion 5% × ₹199/mo = **₹10/user/mo** blended.
- **≈ ₹118/active user/mo** gross revenue potential (before infra + CAC).
- Infra cost per active user (see `business/cost-model.md`) must stay **well under** this — the entire unit-economics question.

## 4. Why India-first, then global
| India-first advantage | Global-later path |
|---|---|
| High return pain + price sensitivity = strong pull | Same fit problem exists everywhere |
| Body diversity underserved by US-centric tools | Avatar/fit engine generalizes |
| Multi-app behavior = cross-store thesis strongest | SEA, Middle East, then West |
| Lower CAC via affiliate/organic | Partnerships travel with brands |

**Expansion sequence:** India → SEA/Middle East (similar dynamics) → global brand-SDK (B2B travels faster than consumer).

## 5. Market timing (why now)
- Generative try-on quality crossed the usability threshold (2024–2025).
- Open perception models + hosted inference = low build barrier for a startup.
- DPDP Act (2023, Rules 2025) creates a **trust moat opportunity** for a privacy-first entrant while raising the bar for careless competitors.
- Retailer appetite to cut returns is high.

## 6. Risks to the market thesis
- Google/Amazon could bundle "good-enough" cross-store try-on → we must own **fit + outfit depth + India specificity**.
- Affiliate economics could compress. → diversify to Pro + B2B SDK.
- Consumer trust in uploading body photos may be lower than assumed. → privacy-first design is a market-entry requirement, not a nicety.
