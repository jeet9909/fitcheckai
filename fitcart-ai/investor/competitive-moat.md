# Investor — Competitive Moat

> **Honest thesis:** the try-on *model* is not the moat — it is commoditizing (Google, open models). FitCart's defensibility is the **data flywheel + structural neutrality + trust brand + B2B optionality**. If a pitch claims "our AI try-on is the moat," it's weak. Ours is deeper.

## 1. The four moats
```mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[1. Fit-data flywheel\n(network effect)] --> M[Durable moat]
    B[2. Structural neutrality\n(cross-store)] --> M
    C[3. Trust & privacy brand\n(India body-data)] --> M
    D[4. B2B SDK optionality\n(sell the shovels)] --> M
```

### Moat 1 — Fit-data flywheel (the real one)
Every try-on + purchase + **post-purchase fit-feedback** improves per-brand, per-category, per-body-type fit prediction. Accuracy compounds with usage → better predictions → more trust → more usage. **A new entrant starts at zero data; we compound.** This is a genuine network/data effect and the strongest moat.

### Moat 2 — Structural neutrality
FitCart routes shoppers to *whichever* store is best. **Marketplaces structurally cannot build this** — Myntra won't build a tool that sends users to Ajio. That neutral cross-store lane is ours by construction, not by cleverness.

### Moat 3 — Trust & privacy brand
Body data + DPDP (2025) create a **trust premium**. The brand that is demonstrably privacy-first with body data earns loyalty a careless competitor can't buy back after a breach. First-mover trust in a newly-regulated market is defensible.

### Moat 4 — B2B SDK optionality
The differentiated fit engine can be licensed to retailers/brands (validated appetite: Walmart/Zeekit, Reactive Reality). This is **high-margin, sticky, and a hedge** against big-tech consumer competition — if the consumer lane gets crowded, we sell the infrastructure.

## 2. What is NOT a moat (state plainly)
- Generative try-on quality (commoditizing — Google leads).
- The app UI (copyable).
- Cross-store aggregation *alone* (others could aggregate) — it's the **combination** with fit-data + neutrality that defends.

## 3. Copy-resistance analysis
| Attacker | Can they copy? | What blunts them |
|---|---|---|
| Google/Amazon | Technically yes | Our fit-data depth + India specificity + neutrality; pivot to B2B |
| Marketplaces | Only within own catalog | Can't be neutral cross-store |
| New startups | Yes, but from zero data | Our compounding fit dataset + trust brand |
| B2B incumbents (Reactive Reality) | In B2B, yes | Our consumer data loop feeds better fit than pure-B2B rivals |

## 4. Moat build order (matches roadmap)
1. **MVP/V2:** start the fit-data flywheel (feedback loop from day one) + trust brand.
2. **V2:** calibration per brand → visible accuracy edge.
3. **V3:** partnerships (cart-sync/assets) + **B2B SDK** monetizing the moat.
